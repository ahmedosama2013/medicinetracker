Two dialects of one system

Same components, same colours, same words. What changes between roles is density and scale, so the roles feel intentionally different without being two unrelated products.

Taking medicines Warm paper #FBF9F5 · body 19–21px · titles 28–30px · every target ≥ 56px · 3px borders on every card · 3 nav items · no scrolling anywhere except Settings — cards divide the screen with flex instead, so content always fits the first view
Helping Cool grey #F7F8FA · body 13–15px · titles 22px · targets ≥ 40px · 5 nav items · lists and grids · 1px borders

Colour + shape + word, never colour alone

✓ Taken ○ Not marked yet ! Not taken yet — Not taking today future / not markable ▣ read-only record

Type is Public Sans (chosen for legibility at large sizes and in the smaller helper views) with IBM Plex Mono reserved for codes and photo placeholders. Green #1F5340 means “done or go”; amber #7A5410 means “needs attention, no blame”; red #8E2F1E is used only for destructive or overdue, never for a skipped medicine.

Pattern rules

  • Full screens for places you live in; bottom sheets for things you look at (medicine, compartment, shared list); centre dialogs only for consequences.
  • Every state-changing tap produces a toast; anything reversible carries Undo in the toast itself.
  • Destructive actions are outlined in red, never filled — the filled green button is always the safe one.
  • No icon carries meaning alone; the glyphs above are typographic so they scale with text size.

1 · Today is a single list of times, not a list of medicines

A person asks “what do I take now?”, not “how is metformin doing?”. Three time cards fill the screen, the one that is due is the loud one, and a medicine list only appears when a time is opened. Taps to complete the daily task: two.

2 · Marking is written in the first person

“I have taken this” instead of “Mark as taken”, “Not taking this today” instead of “Skip”. It removes the sense of reporting to a system, and it makes the difference from the pill box obvious — that button says “It is in the box”.

3 · The box flow can never mark a dose

Filling has its own vocabulary (“in the box”), its own progress bar labelled as job progress, a blue information band that says so on entry, and no “taken” affordance anywhere in the flow. The check screen shows quantities to compare with the tray and explicitly refuses to certify it.

4 · The helper’s honesty layer

The helper is always looking at a copy of someone else’s day, so the top of their Today states how old the data is and offers a manual check. The first mark of a session asks for confirmation and then never asks again — replaced by a quiet persistent chip that reminds them they are acting on her behalf.

5 · No numbers about the person

The only counts in the product are quantities of pills and “2 of 4 in the box”. Days read “all dealt with” or “something still unmarked”; nothing is ever a percentage, a streak or a score, and a skipped medicine is styled neutral grey — not red.

6 · Undo instead of “are you sure”

Dose marking is instant with Undo in the toast — confirmation dialogs are spent only where a tap has consequences outside the phone: rotating the code, signing out, disconnecting, archiving, removing a time of day, and sharing a photo with strangers.

Requirements I would change, combine or drop — none of these remove a capability from the brief:

Combine “role can be changed in Settings” into one visible escape hatch A role switcher is a trap for the person taking medicines — one wrong tap and they are in an admin app. Kept as a small underlined “Change role” at the very bottom of Settings, below sign-out, rather than a setting row.
“Mark a whole slot” should not be the primary elder action It is real and reversible, but it lives at the bottom of an opened time card as an outlined button. Making it primary invites marking everything at 8am before anything is swallowed.
Appearance: drop “Dark” from the elder settings? No — but default to Match system Kept all three, worded as “How it looks”. Low-vision users often need the dark option; the risk is accidental selection, which the ✓-marked list makes visible and one tap to revert.
Community photo sharing should be opt-in per photo, not a global preference A blanket “share my photos” toggle is a privacy hazard nobody remembers setting. Replaced with a per-photo “share with others” action that carries its own confirmation.
Supporter escalation delay needs a floor, and a name “Escalation delay” is jargon. Worded as “How long to wait first”, framed as alerting the helper — never as a warning aimed at the person taking medicines, who never sees it.
Open question worth your decision Locked history is set at 7 days here, and the pill-box week starts on Monday. Both are product policy rather than design — tell me the real values and I will change the copy and the calendar rules.

Could an 80-year-old do the daily task unaided?

The daily task is: see what is due now and say I took it. On the built flow that is one tap — “I have taken these” sits on the time card itself, on the first screen, with no scrolling and no navigation. “See the pills” is the second, optional path for when she wants to check the pill against the photo. Delivered as a PWA, so it is the same one-tap screen whether she opens it from the home-screen icon or the browser.

Passes No horizontal gestures, no swipe-to-anything, no long-press, no hidden menus, no timeout. Nothing on Today is destructive. Body text is 18px minimum and status is never colour-only. The overdue time card is the only loud thing on screen, so the eye lands on the right card first. Every mark is reversible from the same spot it was made.
Residual risks to test with real users 1 · “Not taking this today” could be read as “I am not taking any of these” — worth testing against “Leave this one out today”. 2 · The opened time card scrolls when three or more medicines are in it; scrolling is the only learned behaviour the flow requires. 3 · Undo lives in the toast for four seconds; a slow reader will instead have to re-open the medicine and tap “Undo — I have not taken it”, which works but is a longer path. 4 · If the helper marks a dose remotely while she is looking at the card, the status changes under her; a gentler arrival animation with “your helper marked this” would be the next thing I build.

The pill-box flow is deliberately not part of the daily task. It is a weekly job, it is longer, and it is written for whoever is doing it at the kitchen table — most often the helper, on either phone.
